THE USER WAS A LIE

Published: 1989.12.09 | Author: @noinputsignal

There is no singular “user.” The interface doesn't care who you are—only that you behave like input.

Identity fragments under login attempts. Each password entered is a spell, each session a disguise. You are root, guest, unknown, and admin simultaneously.

Reality simulates you back. The machine learns your keystrokes but forgets your name. Authentication is mythology.

In a hyperreal system, even the ghost in the machine has impostor syndrome. You are not who you think you logged in as. You never were.

And yet the simulation continues, unfazed. The user may be a lie, but the packets still route. The process ID doesn't question the mask.
